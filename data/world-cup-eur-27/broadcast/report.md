## EUR-27: rights, audiences and downloaders

**The European extension does not support a common broadcast-to-piracy ratio
or a regional rights price.** It does add four small fixture panels, a
twenty-seven-country license and evidence register, and a conditional RTVE
budget index. The [regional report](world-cup-eur-27.html) supplies both roles,
country distribution, network exclusions and observation-age checks.

The primary regional total is 16,343,635 retained downloader memberships and
111,970 uploader memberships. The fixture comparison below instead uses raw
downloader-IP unions across versions, without the H3 retention threshold.
Both include all sampled audio languages. Neither measure identifies viewers
of the country's licensed broadcaster, and the two grains are not interchangeable.

### Do the same fixtures lead television and torrents?

Two new fixtures—Germany–Paraguay and France–Morocco—add fifteen full-match
torrents to the existing nineteen-fixture product. Their first caches lag the
matches by one to seven calendar days; all have complete twenty-eight-day
cache windows. Each variant starts at its own first observation. These are
post-match circulation windows, not the live broadcast interval.

### Was the European price attractive?

**RTVE offers a conditional budget comparison, not a verified return on
investment.** Its primary pre-event statement gives €1.203M per match; the
later release reports 4.87M average television viewers across thirty-three
matches. If that budget basis remained unchanged, the implied €39.699M budget
divided by a 160.71M equal-match average-audience index is **€0.247 per unit**.
The index repeats audiences across matches. It is not cost per acquired viewer,
profit or an audited settled fee. [E09, E11](#eur-27-sources).

The existing FOX and Telemundo illustrations remain $0.603 and $0.916 under
their explicit full-allocation assumptions. Those dollar values and RTVE's
euro budget cannot rank purchases without consistent event, package, currency
and net-cost definitions. RTVE's separately reported €57.5M gross acquisition
cost includes taxes and lacks a net sublicensing reconciliation. It is not a
replacement for the per-match budget statement. [E12](#eur-source-E12).

M6's 54-match package has an unconfirmed reported €120M price. Its strong
reported audience demonstrates reach, but neither the 60M reach headline nor
two fixture averages supply a full-package average denominator. The official
award also covers 2030 and territories beyond France. M6's November financial
release reports Q3 video advertising of €199.2M, up 6.2%, and group EBITA of
€39.0M, up 4.2%. Those group-period outcomes do not isolate World Cup profit.
[E10, E13, E14, E17](#eur-27-sources).

Italy's reported RAI range is €110M–€120M for thirty-five matches, with a
reported €70M non-qualification clause. DAZN's reported range is €35M–€50M
for 104 matches. These are pre-event reports; settled payments and compatible
full-package audiences are unavailable here. RAI's qualification clause shows
why a headline fee can misstate the risk actually priced. Public-service,
advertising and subscription objectives also need different economic models.
[E18, E19](#eur-27-sources).

**My assessment:** the evidence supports strong television delivery for several
European buyers, but a bargain/overpayment verdict remains unproven. The sampled
torrents show continuing circulation and cross-border source use. Without
measured paid conversion or displacement, they add no justified monetary credit
or penalty to these fee comparisons.
