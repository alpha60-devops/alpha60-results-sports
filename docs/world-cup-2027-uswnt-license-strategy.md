<section class="brief-section" id="executive-decision" aria-labelledby="executive-decision-title"><p class="brief-kicker">Executive decision</p><h2 id="executive-decision-title">Give the buyer a reason to follow the players home</h2><p>Jessica Berman should ask Netflix for a paid series that introduces players through their clubs and brings viewers back to the NWSL when its season resumes at the end of July. The first question for a buyer is where it will put that first club fixture in front of the person who watched the story.</p>
<p><strong>The proposed offer.</strong> Eight 12-minute club features, each with two short promotional edits and a link to the featured club’s next broadcast. Quote $600,000 for English in the U.S. and Canada, U.S. Spanish and Canadian French. Invite a separately cleared Mexican Spanish edition. Offer all three countries for $660,000 if one order saves $30,000 in delivery costs. These are opening fee targets for one 2027 window, contingent on access and approvals.</p>
<p><strong>Why the league has something to sell.</strong> Eighteen of the 23 players on the November qualifying roster play for 13 NWSL clubs. Club cameras can show what a viewer would otherwise miss. A Washington feature can follow Trinity Rodman from the training ground to a teammate’s account of how a play was built. A Kansas City feature can tell that story from the other side.</p>
<p><strong>The boundary of the deal.</strong> FIFA licensed the tournament. Netflix has U.S. and Canadian exclusivity. Berman can negotiate club access, a commission and promotion of the league restart. Live matches, FIFA highlights and national-team access require separate authority. Budget zero independent NWSL revenue from selling those match rights.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Berman’s opening.</strong> “We can introduce your audience to the clubs these players come home to. What placement can you guarantee when our season starts up again?”</p>
</blockquote>

<section class="brief-section" id="championship" aria-labelledby="championship-title"><p class="brief-kicker">The league business</p><h2 id="championship-title">What the Washington–Kansas City final brings to the table</h2><p>Dan Shanoff’s November 19 MoneyCall framed the final as both a showcase for players such as Rodman and Temwa Chawinga and a prelude to Berman’s next media negotiation. Kansas City then beat Washington 2–1 at Audi Field. That gives a producer a story with a result, two club perspectives and a reason to return next season.</p>
<p><strong>Start with the people on both sides of the match.</strong> Washington’s Rodman and Tara Rudd and Kansas City’s Michelle Cooper and Croix Bethune are on the November U.S. roster. Chawinga, a Malawi international, is a reason the club story works even when a buyer’s favorite national team exits. Obtain participation agreements before naming talent in a delivery guarantee.</p>
<p><strong>Give the buyer two honest comparisons.</strong> The 2025 NWSL final averaged 1.184 million viewers on CBS. Sports Business Journal reported that the four-year domestic league package announced in 2023 averaged $60 million a year including production and marketing. The former measures one live match. The latter combines several kinds of consideration for a season. Neither sets a price for eight short features.</p>
<p><strong>Use the 2027 calendar as a bargaining asset.</strong> The league plans a pause during the World Cup and a return at the end of July. Ask for the right to promote a confirmed local fixture on each service, a scheduled return promo and a report from the buyer. Keep any option on the next league-rights cycle out of this short commission.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Berman on the renewal.</strong> “A tournament partner can show us which viewers choose club soccer a week later. I want the evidence in hand when we price the next domestic deal.”</p>
</blockquote>

<p>The 2023 U.S.–Netherlands group match averaged 6.429 million on FOX and 1.51 million across Spanish-language outlets. That single U.S. evening supports testing an 18% Spanish share in the base case. It cannot calibrate Canada, a full tournament or relative fees. The French and smaller-language weights are negotiating sensitivities pending buyer research and confirmation of language scope in Canada. Kickoff time and opponents also change the value of a fixture.</p>
<p><strong>What might the USWNT matches account for?</strong> Give an ordinary game a weight of one. If the U.S. qualifies, three group games at twice ordinary value imply $17.9 million of the $200 million. Five games at triple value imply $40.5 million. Seven at quadruple value imply $65.9 million. The calculation is $200 million times the weighted U.S. games divided by the weight of all 64 games. Without qualification the U.S.-match component is zero. These are alternative allocations within the same contract, with no published USWNT fee.</p>

<p><strong>Define the return measure.</strong> Among eligible accounts that watched a feature, measure the share starting a licensed NWSL match within seven days of the first available restart fixture. Compare against eligible accounts in a holdout where feasible. Publish the sample sizes and distinguish direct tune-in clicks from match starts. Where services cannot match accounts with permission, use aggregate market lift and label the attribution limit.</p>
<p><strong>Decision for the next cycle.</strong> Retain an edition that meets its cash hurdle and demonstrates incremental club viewing. Requote a promising track that lacks margin. Return unexercised territory options and take anonymized results into the next league-rights negotiation.</p>
