# Latin America and South America

Brazil leads the observed regional distribution. Hosting shares are low; Spanish and Portuguese source coverage remains a priority.

<a id="southern-results"></a>

## Regional results and boundaries

**Brazil leads both regional views.** Across the full World Cup snapshot, Latin America and the Caribbean account for 5,184,562 downloader memberships; South America accounts for 3,559,156. Brazil contributes 2,509,871. Hosting flags cover only 1.4% and 1.6% of the two regional downloader totals, far below the US shares in the companion network study.

Sampling began **June 29, 2026 — Round of 32** and runs through November 3. These are observations of sampled peer-to-peer distribution, including later circulation of earlier matches. They do not measure the tournament's live audience, national fandom or completed viewing.

“Latin America” uses **UN M49 Latin America and the Caribbean (419)**: 52 countries or areas across Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean and South America. **South America (005)** is its 16-country-or-area subset. This geographic definition includes territories and places whose main languages are neither Spanish nor Portuguese. South America is already inside the first total; **do not add the two regions together**. The USA and Canada are outside both. [Official UN M49 definition](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/overview/)

## Implications for the next sample

Brazil's large observed distribution, combined with absent identified Portuguese audio, makes Portuguese discovery a clear coverage priority. Track the search source, language claim, discovery date, first observed cache and validated audio tracks for each new hash. Compare versions of the same match over the same availability window before interpreting a regional preference.

Preserve countries and territories with zero retained counts, and keep small uploader denominators visible. A zero after the H3r8 threshold does not prove no activity. Compare per-torrent and aggregate retention, and retain the H3r5 sensitivity when examining sparse locations. The same cells, addresses and media can contribute to overlapping regions and flags, so neither should be treated as independent populations.
